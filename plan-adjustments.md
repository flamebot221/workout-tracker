# Workout plan adjustments: what to observe and when to change

## Start as written

Keep the same workout split and exercises for the first **2–4 weeks**. In week 1, choose controlled weights that leave **2–3 reps in reserve**: you should finish challenged, but not exhausted.

The purpose of the first weeks is to learn good form, build consistency, and collect useful information. Do not change exercises just because they feel unfamiliar or difficult at first.

## When to adjust

| What you observe | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| You complete every set at the top of its rep range with good form | Add the smallest available weight next time you do that exercise. |
| You cannot reach the minimum listed reps for two sessions in a row | Lower the weight for that exercise by 5–10%, then build up again. |
| Severe soreness lasts more than 2–3 days, sleep worsens, or you feel constantly fatigued | Keep the same weekly schedule, but remove one set from each exercise for one week. |
| Knee, shoulder, back, or chest pain occurs (not normal muscle soreness) | Stop that movement. Ask a qualified trainer to check your form; seek clinical advice for persistent, sharp, or concerning pain. |
| Your average weight does not drop for two full weeks and you followed the plan | Keep the gym plan unchanged. Add 1,500–2,000 daily steps **or** reduce food by about 150–200 kcal/day. |
| You lose more than about 1 kg/week for two weeks and energy or strength is falling | Add about 150–200 kcal/day, prioritizing protein and minimally processed food. |
| Waist measurement is decreasing and strength is stable or improving | Change nothing. The plan is working. |

## Track every two weeks

- Average of 3–4 morning weigh-ins each week
- Waist measurement at navel level
- Front and side progress photos in similar lighting
- Gym log: exercise, weight, reps, and sets
- Sleep, energy, soreness, and any pain

## Key rule

Change weights, food intake, steps, or short-term training volume when the observations call for it. Avoid changing the workout exercises or the weekly split without a clear reason. Consistency is the priority.
